# CAN ECU Simulator — 2012 Toyota Corolla (E140) 1.8L

An ESP32 + MCP2515 bench simulator that puts realistic traffic on a 500 kbit/s
CAN bus and answers OBD-II and UDS diagnostic requests.

The traffic is generated by an actual vehicle model rather than by waveforms.
A driver model works the pedals to track a target speed; the engine makes
torque against a real road load; the transmission shifts on a load-dependent
schedule through a torque converter. Every broadcast signal and every
diagnostic response is read out of that one model, so throttle, RPM, road
speed, airflow, temperature and fuel consumption stay causally consistent with
each other — which is what distinguishes this from a bus traffic generator.

## Layout

| File | Role |
| --- | --- |
| `include/vehicle_params.h` | Published physical parameters for the real car. One source of truth. |
| `include/vehicle_model.h` / `src/vehicle_model.cpp` | Engine, torque converter, gearbox, chassis, thermal, fuel and electrical model. No Arduino dependency, so it runs on the host. |
| `include/driver_model.h` / `src/driver_model.cpp` | Closed-loop driver and drive-cycle scenarios. |
| `include/obd_pids.h` | The sparse set of mode 01 PIDs this vehicle actually supports. |
| `include/obd_server.h` / `src/obd_server.cpp` | ISO 15765-2 transport plus OBD-II modes 01–0A and UDS 22/3E. |
| `src/main.cpp` | Broadcast scheduling, frame packing, serial console. |

`vehicle_model` and `driver_model` are free of Arduino headers on purpose: the
physics can be compiled and validated on a workstation without hardware.

## Vehicle model

Powertrain is a 2ZR-FE (1.8 L, 173 Nm @ 4400, 98 kW @ 6000) driving a U340E
4-speed automatic (2.847 / 1.552 / 1.000 / 0.700, 3.943 final) on 205/55R16
tyres.

- **Engine** — WOT torque curve interpolated over 13 breakpoints, scaled by a
  non-linear throttle-plate-to-cylinder-charge map, minus a speed-dependent
  friction and pumping term. A PI idle-air controller holds the target idle
  speed, which is raised when the engine is cold.
- **Torque converter** — signed quadratic fluid coupling with torque
  multiplication below the coupling point, plus a lockup clutch with speed
  hysteresis. Because the coupling is signed, engine braking falls out of the
  model rather than being special-cased.
- **Transmission** — upshift and downshift thresholds both rise with load, so
  the car upshifts near 2000 rpm cruising and holds to ~5800 rpm at full
  throttle. Shifts take time, cut torque, and shorten under load.
- **Chassis** — `F = tractive − aero − rolling − grade − brake`, integrated
  with an effective mass that accounts for rotating inertia. Wheel speeds carry
  driven-axle slip and inside/outside turn differences.
- **Air and fuel** — speed-density airflow (`MAP · Vd · N · VE / R·T`), fuel
  burn from airflow through the commanded mixture, closed-loop trim behaviour,
  deceleration fuel cut-off, and a lazy downstream O2 sensor behind a healthy
  catalyst.
- **Thermal** — heat in scales with load and engine speed, heat out depends on
  whether the thermostat has opened and how much air is passing the radiator.
  The cooling fan cycles on a hysteresis band. Oil lags coolant and runs hotter
  under sustained load.

| 50 | 49.1 | 3 | 1847 | 11.1% | 32 kPa | 8.8 g/s | 6.05 |
| 70 | 69.1 | 3 | 2228 | 13.5% | 36 kPa | 11.9 g/s | 5.57 |
| 90 | 88.9 | 4 | 2531 | 18.2% | 44 kPa | 16.3 g/s | 5.95 |
| 100 | 99.2 | 4 | 2629 | 21.9% | 49 kPa | 18.9 g/s | 6.22 |
| 120 | 119.2 | 4 | 2787 | 31.8% | 62 kPa | 25.0 g/s | 6.87 |

Against the real car: 100 km/h in top gear is 2317 rpm geometrically and the
model sits at 2629 including converter slip on grade; published highway
consumption is ~6.2 L/100 km against 6.22 here; idle airflow is ~3 g/s against
3.2. Coast-down from 100 km/h takes 46 s in gear with DFCO active throughout.
Warmup from 24 °C reaches 88 °C in about 4.5 minutes and then holds on the
thermostat.

Known deviation: wide-open-throttle 0–100 km/h comes out at 12.6 s against a
published ~10.5 s. The road-load and torque figures are the published ones, so
this has been left alone rather than fudged to hit a single number.

## Diagnostics

Four modules answer on their own addresses, and a functional request at 0x7DF
fans out to every module that implements the service — which is how a scan tool
discovers what is on the bus.

| Module | Request | Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ECM | 0x7E0 | 0x7E8 |
| TCM | 0x7E1 | 0x7E9 |
| ABS | 0x7E2 | 0x7EA |
| BCM | 0x7E3 | 0x7EB |

Services: **01** (current data), **02** (freeze frame), **03** (stored DTCs),
**04** (clear), **06** (monitor results), **07** (pending DTCs), **09**
(VIN/CAL-ID/CVN/ECU name), **0A** (permanent DTCs), **22** (read data by
identifier), **3E** (tester present, including the suppress-response bit).

The transport is real ISO 15765-2: multi-frame responses send a first frame and
then **wait for the tester's flow control**, honouring its block size and STmin.
Multi-frame requests are reassembled and acknowledged with flow control. A new
request cancels anything still in flight for that module.

Mode 01 advertises a **sparse, honest** supported-PID bitmap
(`BE 3F B8 13` / `80 07 F0 11` / `FE D0 80 00`) derived from a single table in
`obd_pids.h`. PIDs this engine has no hardware for — bank 2 trims, fuel rail
pressure, oil temperature — are absent, and an unsupported PID returns
NRC 0x31 rather than fabricated data.

## Broadcast map

Periods carry ±4% jitter. DLC varies by frame, and only the frames that would
carry one on a real bus have a rolling counter and CRC.

| ID | Period | DLC | Contents | Protected |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0x0AA | 20 ms | 8 | RPM, throttle, pedal, load, status | counter + CRC |
| 0x0B4 | 20 ms | 8 | Four wheel speeds, ¼ km/h per bit | — |
| 0x0B0 | 50 ms | 7 | Coolant, intake, MAP, MAF, gear, flags | — |
| 0x1D2 | 50 ms | 6 | Gear, turbine RPM, lockup/shift flags | counter + CRC |
| 0x260 | 25 ms | 5 | Steering angle, brake | counter + CRC |
| 0x3B3 | 100 ms | 8 | Fuel level, trims, lambda, O2, EGR | — |
| 0x2A0 | 100 ms | 8 | Odometer, speed, runtime, consumption | — |
| 0x3C8 | 100 ms | 4 | Battery millivolts, alternator | — |
| 0x620 | 200 ms | 5 | Ambient, doors, lights, HVAC | — |
| 0x385 | 500 ms | 8 | Four tyre pressures, temperature, status | counter + CRC |

Identifiers and payload layouts are plausible for the platform but are **not**
reverse-engineered from a real Toyota, and are not claimed to match one.

## Serial console

115200 baud.

```
status                 current vehicle state
scenario <name>        trip | city | highway | idle | manual | keyoff
speed <kph>            target speed (switches to manual)
dtc set <code>         store a confirmed fault, e.g. dtc set P0171
dtc pending <code>     store a pending fault
dtc list               show stored faults
dtc clear              clear faults (permanent codes survive)
help                   this list
```

Setting a confirmed emissions DTC illuminates the MIL in mode 01 PID 01,
captures a freeze frame, and makes the code permanent so it survives a mode 04
clear — the same way the real ECU behaves.

## Bugs fixed from the previous firmware

- **The odometer could never move.** It accumulated into a `float` holding
  124532.4, where one ULP is 7.8 m; a 50 ms step at 58 km/h is 0.8 m, so every
  addition was silently discarded. Verified frozen at 0.000 km over ten
  simulated minutes. Distance is now accumulated in metres and handed to a
  `double` a whole metre at a time.
- **The fuel tank emptied in about four seconds.** The level was truncated back
  into a `uint8_t` every tick, so 81.99994 became 81, then 80.9999 became 80 —
  losing 1% per 50 ms step. Fuel is now tracked as litres in a float and burnt
  from actual airflow.
- **Rear-right wheel speed was destroyed on every frame.** 0x0B4 packed four
  16-bit wheel speeds across all eight bytes and then stamped a counter and CRC
  into bytes 6–7, overwriting the RR value. That frame now carries wheel data
  only, which is also what the real one does.
- **Two of the four TPMS temperatures were destroyed the same way.** The frame
  has been repacked so the counter and CRC have somewhere to live.
- **Warmup counter incremented ~20× per event**, because the trigger condition
  held for a full second at a 50 ms tick rate. It is now edge-triggered once
  per run.

## Building

```bash
~/.platformio/penv/bin/pio run
```

The physics and diagnostics can be exercised on the host without hardware —
`vehicle_model.cpp` and `driver_model.cpp` compile standalone, and
`obd_server.cpp` needs only small stand-ins for `Arduino.h` and `mcp2515.h`.
